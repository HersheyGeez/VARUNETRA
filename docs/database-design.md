# VARUNETRA - Database Design

## Database Overview

The VARUNETRA database is designed to support water resource monitoring, community reporting, sensor-based data collection, flood risk assessment, and alert generation.

The system follows a Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) model and integrates data from both community users and IoT sensor networks.

---

# User Roles

## Admin

Administrators have elevated privileges and can:

* Manage users
* Manage water resources
* Manage danger zones
* Verify or reject reports
* Configure alerts
* Access analytics and system-wide data

## Member

Members can:

* Register and login
* View water resources
* Submit reports
* Add water resources
* Upload images
* View alerts and danger zones

---

# Database Entities

## Roles

### Purpose

Stores user role definitions for Role-Based Access Control.

### Relationships

* One Role can have many Users.

---

## Users

### Purpose

Stores information about registered users of the platform.

### Relationships

* Belongs to one Role.
* Can create multiple Water Sources.
* Can submit multiple Reports.
* Can upload multiple Images.

---

## WaterSources

### Purpose

Stores information about water resources mapped within the system.

### Examples

* Pond
* Well
* Reservoir
* Stream
* River
* Lake
* Tank
* Canal

### Relationships

* Created by one User.
* Can contain zero or more Sensor Nodes.
* Can contain multiple Images.
* Can contain multiple Reports.
* Can be associated with Alerts.

### Design Decision

A Water Source can exist without any Sensor Node.

This allows community-driven mapping even when no hardware has been deployed.

---

## WaterSourceImages

### Purpose

Stores image references associated with Water Sources.

### Relationships

* Belongs to one Water Source.
* Uploaded by one User.

### Design Decision

Images are stored as URLs or file paths rather than binary data.

---

## SensorNodes

### Purpose

Represents physical monitoring units deployed at a Water Source.

### Examples

* ESP32 Monitoring Unit
* Reservoir Monitoring Node
* River Monitoring Node

### Relationships

* Belongs to one Water Source.
* Generates multiple Sensor Readings.

### Design Decision

One Sensor Node can generate multiple measurements including:

* Water Level
* Rainfall
* Flow Rate

---

## SensorReadings

### Purpose

Stores historical sensor measurements fetched from ThingSpeak.

### Measurements

* Water Level
* Rainfall
* Flow Rate

### Relationships

* Belongs to one Sensor Node.

---

## Reports

### Purpose

Stores community-generated reports related to water resources and environmental conditions.

### Examples

* Water Shortage
* Contamination
* Overflow
* Blocked Channel
* Damaged Infrastructure
* Flood Warning
* Illegal Dumping

### Relationships

* Submitted by one User.
* May belong to a Water Source.
* May contain Images.

### Design Decision

Reports can exist without being linked to a Water Source.

This allows reporting of newly discovered incidents and emergency situations.

---

## ReportImages

### Purpose

Stores images uploaded as supporting evidence for Reports.

### Relationships

* Belongs to one Report.
* Uploaded by one User.

---

## DangerZones

### Purpose

Stores flood-prone and high-risk geographical regions.

### Examples

* Floodplain Areas
* River Overflow Zones
* High-Risk Stream Regions

### Relationships

* Can generate multiple Alerts.

### Design Decision

Danger Zones are represented using:

* Latitude
* Longitude
* Radius

instead of complex polygon geometries.

This simplifies implementation and visualization.

---

## Alerts

### Purpose

Stores warnings and notifications generated by the system.

### Sources

* Sensor Data
* Community Reports
* Administrative Actions

### Examples

* High Water Level Alert
* Heavy Rainfall Alert
* Flood Risk Warning
* Water Contamination Alert

### Relationships

* May reference a Water Source.
* May reference a Danger Zone.

# Key Design Decisions

1. Role-Based Access Control is implemented using separate Roles and Users entities.

2. Water Sources can exist without Sensor Nodes.

3. One Sensor Node can generate multiple measurements.

4. Sensor data is fetched from ThingSpeak and stored within the platform database.

5. Images are stored using URLs or file paths rather than binary storage.

6. Reports can be submitted without linking to an existing Water Source.

7. Danger Zones use a center-point and radius representation.

8. Alerts can originate from sensor data, community reports, or administrative actions.
